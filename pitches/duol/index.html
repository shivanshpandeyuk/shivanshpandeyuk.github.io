A team stock pitch — students Kevin Zeng, Ashwin Bindra, Sze Yu Wang and Emilio Lara. The price target and figures below are the team’s estimates at the time of the pitch.

Duolingo (NASDAQ: DUOL) — Long. ‘25 price target $176.13, 85% upside and a 21% IRR [E, original pitch].

The core claim: subscription penetration growth is understated because the Street misunderstands Duolingo’s core customer cohort, and it is set to hit an inflection point rather than plateau.

Business overview

Duolingo is a “freemium” gamified language-learning app with over 500 million downloads globally [U, as stated in the pitch]. Revenue comes from four reporting segments: subscription 72% (annual and monthly subscriptions to Duolingo Plus, where the penetration argument lives), advertising 15% (in-app ads to free users), the Duolingo English Test 10% (an on-demand English-proficiency assessment accepted by 18 of the top-20 US undergraduate programmes, and already about a tenth of the market within two years of launch), and other 3% (in-app purchases — gems, streak repairs, bonuses) [R, revenue split as stated in the pitch].

Best-in-category product

Management runs more than 500 A/B tests a quarter, using the customer base to keep improving the UX and monetisation. That feeds engagement and user growth, which in turn feeds the data moat and spreads R&D across a larger revenue base. Duolingo has gained more than 6% app-download market share since 2018 and is the number-one education app in the world [U, as stated in the pitch].

Thesis 1: misunderstood core customer

The Street reads Duolingo as a COVID darling whose engagement should normalise down after reopening. The pitch argues the opposite: COVID was a headwind for monetisation, not a tailwind. During the pandemic the user mix shifted towards casual learners — new growth was driven by interest in Asian languages, which skew recreational — and only 23% of 2020 incremental users were English learners [R, as stated in the pitch]. That cohort was less monetisable, churned more and bought fewer in-app purchases and subscriptions, which dragged on subscription penetration. Those users have since churned off and are being replaced by “motivated” learners, so monetisation should accelerate rather than decelerate.

Thesis 2: international monetisation

Duolingo IPO’d with no international pricing plan, so users outside the US were under-monetised and churned quickly. Rest-of-world payer penetration sits well below the US. With flexible, region-specific pricing now rolling out — and public comps such as Netflix and Spotify as a reference for monetising other regions — the international base is the final growth lever: “two steps backwards, one leap forward”.

Variance to the Street

In line with sell-side on user growth (MAU and DAU), variant on subscription penetration and subscriber growth: about 280bps above Street on subscribers/MAU and roughly 35% above on end-of-period subscribers by 2026, which flows through to 39% above Street on subscription revenue and 30% above on total revenue by 2026 [E, as stated in the pitch].

Valuation

Blended ‘25 price target of $176.1 (85% upside, 21% IRR), weighting four methods: a Year-5 exit multiple, a DCF, a payer-penetration multiple and a linear regression on freemium comps. Base-case points are $186.53 (exit multiple), $199.83 (DCF), $137.59 (penetration multiple) and $136.85 (regression) [E, original pitch].

Pre-mortem

The pitch’s three failure cases: motivated users do not in fact have a higher propensity to spend; the COVID down-tick in monetisation is true platform churn rather than a shift in user mix; or the international regions are not composed of motivated, monetisable users. The mitigants are the large base of motivated learners using Duolingo for economic mobility, evidence that the casual COVID cohort has already churned off, and the fact that the leading international language studied is English.
