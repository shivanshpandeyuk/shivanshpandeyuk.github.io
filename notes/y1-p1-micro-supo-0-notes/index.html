    Standard consumer-theory definitions here (the axioms of choice; WARP, SARP and GARP) follow Varian, Intermediate Microeconomics, the Part I course text. The supervision questions and feedback are the supervisor’s; the working and notes are the author’s.

    In consumer theory, a binary preference ordering embodies three axioms of choice: it must be complete, reflexive and transitive.

    If preferences adhere to these axioms, then they can be characterized by a utility function.

    If preferences are well-behaved – that is, they are convex and monotonic – then the utility function will generate smooth downward sloping demand curves.

    DIRECTLY REVEALED PREFERRED DEFINITION: An allocation X is directly revealed preferred to a different allocation Y if Y was in the budget set when X was chosen.

    WEAK AXIOM OF REVEALED PREFERENCE (WARP). If allocation X is directly revealed preferred to Y, then Y cannot be directly revealed preferred to X.

    STRONG AXIOM OF REVEALED PREFERENCE (SARP). If allocation X is revealed preferred to Y, then Y will never be revealed preferred to X.

    GENERALIZED AXIOM OF REVEALED PREFERENCE (GARP). If an allocation X is revealed preferred to Y, then Y is never strictly directly revealed preferred to X, that is, X is never strictly within the budget set when Y is chosen.

    But the axioms of choice indicate that what is “rational” is what is consistent, that is, it can be characterized by convex preferences. Hence, whether this benevolent behavior is rational is an empirical question that the experimental economist is perfectly suited to answer.

    FEEDBACK Q1) Completeness - A preferred B, B preferred A or A indifferent B. [this rules out “I don’t know”] Transitivity - A preferred B, B preferred C, A preferred C. [prevents preference cycles] Reflexivity - Bundle A is at least just as good as itself i.e. A weakly preferred to A. is also a condition but this arises as a result of both Completeness and Transitivity holding.

    Q2) Trivial

    Q3) A preference ranking can have any infinite amount of utility functions representing it (any monotonic transformation can be applied). But a utility function can only ever represent a single unique ranking order (this is because there is only one way to order numbers!).

    BOTH must hold for consumer to make a full ranking of all bundles available to them.

    Q4) Goods are the “points” not the tokens, utility is derived from the points not the tokens. (e.g. wine in glass, utility derived from wine so the wine is the good.)

    Q6) The slope of the budget line is the OPPORUNITY COST it is NOT the MRS (which corresponds to preferences).
