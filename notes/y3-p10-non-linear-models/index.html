Lecture Notes

Loosely speaking, a time series may be said to be linear when the impact of a shock is:

  • Proportional to its size
  • Independent of its sign (in an absolute sense it is symmetric)
  • Independent of the current value of the time series. Whenever one of these three properties does not hold, a time series may be said to be non-linear.

ARMA modelling does not fit the bill; it gives us symmetric and proportional responses. But we know for instance the business cycle is assymetric - with short, sharp recessions and long expansions.

Markov Switching Models

Hamilton introduced the idea of using Markov switching models to describe time series. For the simplest such model we might write

yt=μ+ϵty_t = \mu + \epsilon_t yt​=μ+ϵt​

The mean of the process depends on the state of the world, S

μ=μ1St+μ2(1−St)\mu = \mu_1 S_t + \mu_2(1-S_t)μ=μ1​St​+μ2​(1−St​)

St=0,1S_t = 0,1St​=0,1 as per a Markov process, the probability that it takes either value depends on its prior value.

  • p = prob. of staying in state 0, given in 0
  • q = prob. of staying in state 1, given in 1

Clearly we can be more exotic and either introduce further states of the world, or allow the state variable to control not just mean but also variance of shocks or even coefficients.

Choosing a Specification

Unlike linear models where we have the Wold decomposition theorem, **there is very little to guide once one sets out to estimate non-linear models. **(We can’t say it is all ARMA)

That’s why always test if we have non-linearity We really wish we don’t. But if we do test for it using:

  • Ramsey’s RESET test - a simple test
    for non-linear behaviour that essentially augments a regression with powers of the forecast values of
    yty_tyt​ and tests whether their coefficients are zero.
  • **LM tests can be used **against particular alternatives.

Whilst one step ahead forecasting from non-linear models is usually feasible, further ahead forecasting is complicated due to the interaction of the shocks and the non-linearity.

  • As shown with the SETAR case, where yt+2y_{t+2}yt+2​ was going to get an insane volatility because of a minor over-estimate of yt+1y_{t+1}yt+1​ In general in-sample diagnostics are insufficient for choosing a non-linear model because of the dangers of overfitting/specification searches/data mining. Some form of out of sample cross validation should be used
