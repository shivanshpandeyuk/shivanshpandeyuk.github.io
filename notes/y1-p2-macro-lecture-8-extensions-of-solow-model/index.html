Solow model and Convergence In the above case using a sort of cobb-douglas production function shows that growth reduces as capital stock per person increases (the implicatation is there is convergence between capital rich and capital poor countries). Visually: If the world behaves like the Solow model, we should observe convergence if countries differ only with respect to initial capital and share same s, n, δ. Then poor countries should grow faster and we would expect a negative relationship between initial income and growth.

Such convergence is observed when we consider only the cluster of developed economies there is a clear negative relation between GDP/capita and growth: E.g./ The great divergence:

This doesn’t mean the Solow model fails.

  • In samples of countries with similar savings & pop. growth rates, income gaps shrink about 2%/year
  • In larger samples, if one controls for differences in saving, population growth, and human capital, incomes converge by about 2%/year

Consider for example a country being poor due to its low saving rate: What the Solow model really predicts is conditional convergence - countries converge to their own steady states, which are determined by saving, population growth, and productivity. And this prediction comes true in the real world and is consistent with the great divergence.

EVALUATING THE SOLOW MODEL Strengths

  1. Gives us: long run = steady state
  2. The principle of transition dynamics – allows for understanding of differences in growth rates across countries – a country further from k* grows faster Weaknesses
  3. It focuses on investment and capital and so does not provide a theory of sustained long‐run economic growth.
  4. The much more important factor of productivity A is still unexplained
  5. Doesn’t explain why countries have different investment and productivity rates
  6. A more complicated model could endogenise the investment rate

These weaknesses are the precursor to introducing technology in the production function. Either through exogeneous growth theory or endogenous growth theory.
